## Findings (concrete)

### 1. The sea dragon was made to play (KJV)
- **Psalm 104:26** — "There go the ships: there is that leviathan, whom thou hast made to play therein."
- Strong's lists Leviathan (H3882) as a "serpent," a sea monster or dragon. The verb is **sachaq** (H7832): to laugh, play, make merry, rejoice.
- The Cambridge commentary's reading: the sea is "the playground of the mighty monsters," not a cage. The scariest creature in scripture is described as something God shaped *for* frolic.
- Source: [Psalm 104:26 lexicon](https://biblehub.com/lexicon/psalms/104-26.htm), [commentaries](https://biblehub.com/commentaries/psalms/104-26.htm).

### 2. Wisdom plays too, and she does it daily
- **Proverbs 8:30–31** — "Then I was by him, as one brought up with him: and I was daily his delight, rejoicing always before him; Rejoicing in the habitable part of his earth; and my delights were with the sons of men."
- "Rejoicing" here is the *same word*, sachaq. The Hebrew for "daily" is literally "day, day" (yom yom). Wisdom at creation is pictured playing before God, day after day.
- **Zechariah 8:5** — the restored city's sign of peace: "the streets of the city shall be full of boys and girls playing in the streets thereof."
- Source: [Strong's 7832, sachaq](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/7832.htm).

### 3. The dragon and the pearl: a game, not a hoard
- Chinese dragons are shown "cavorting among clouds and playing with a pearl." The Met's journal notes the Chinese dragon is "far from being the virgin-devouring, treasure-guarding monster of European medieval romances." ([Met Museum Journal, *The Dragon and the Pearl*](https://resources.metmuseum.org/resources/metpublications/pdf/The_Dragon_and_the_Pearl_The_Metropolitan_Museum_Journal_v_26_1991.pdf))
- A classic jade-carving pattern is literally named "a dragon frolicking with a pearl." The flaming pearl stands for wisdom and compassion, and two dragons chasing it is read as a shared search for truth. ([Flora Lamson Hewlett Library, Berkeley](https://www.artworkarchive.com/profile/gtu/artwork/pearl-of-wisdom-graduate-theological-union?collection=sacred-world-art-collection))
- In the dragon dance, one person holds the pearl and plays with *two* dragons, who push it back and forth between them. The motif is about 3,000 years old in folk tradition and shows up as a Tang-era pattern by ~600 AD.

### 4. The oldest board games were passage games
- **Senet** (Egypt, by ~2686 BC, maybe older) means "passing." In the New Kingdom it became a picture of the soul's journey: some squares were hazards, some gave free turns, and the worst pitfall was "the primeval Waters of Chaos," where a piece was lost. Tutankhamun was buried with five game boxes. ([The Met: Senet and Twenty Squares](https://www.metmuseum.org/articles/ancient-egypt-board-games), [Piccione on senet](https://piccionep.people.charleston.edu/piccione_senet.pdf))
- **Royal Game of Ur** (~2600 BC, British Museum). Irving Finkel reconstructed its rules from a Babylonian tablet written ~177 BC (BM 33333B) by the scribe Itti-Marduk-balāṭu, copying an earlier scribe. The tablet also links the game to fortune-telling and to astronomy. Someone wrote the rules down so later players wouldn't lose them. ([Wikipedia: Royal Game of Ur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Game_of_Ur))

### 5. Play as hallowed ground (philosophy thread)
- Johan Huizinga, *Homo Ludens* (1938): "Just as there is no formal difference between play and ritual, so the 'consecrated spot' cannot be formally distinguished from the play-ground. The arena, the card-table, the magic circle, the temple, the stage, the screen... are all in form and function play-grounds... hallowed, within which special rules obtain. All are temporary worlds within the ordinary world."
- And: play "creates order, is order. Into an imperfect world and into the confusion of life it brings a temporary, a limited perfection."
- Source: [Magic circle (games)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_circle_(games)).
